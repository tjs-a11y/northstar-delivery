How freight class is calculated

Less-than-truckload (LTL) carriers price freight partly by its density — how much a shipment weighs for the space it takes up. Denser freight is cheaper to classify; light, bulky freight costs more because it fills the trailer without filling the weight.

The three steps

  • Volume: length × width × height (in inches) ÷ 1,728 = cubic feet per unit.
  • Density: total weight ÷ total cubic feet = pounds per cubic foot.
  • Class: that density maps to one of 18 NMFC classes, from 50 (dense) to 500 (very light).

Why your quote may show a different class

For many commodities the National Motor Freight Classification sets the class by density alone, but some are classed by stowability, ease of handling, or liability (value and fragility) instead. If your item has a fixed NMFC number, the carrier will use it. This calculator gives you the density-based starting point so there are no surprises when you request a quote.
