Diesel Eases to $6.292 as CDL Enforcement Sweeps 15 States

Diesel slips week over week, a multistate CDL crackdown nets 40-plus drivers, and carrier earnings show cost pressure persisting.

Diesel fell 6.0 cents to $6.292 a gallon this week, but it is still up 9.3 cents over four weeks, so fuel relief remains thin.

Diesel Slips, Still Elevated

  • $6.292 per gallon is the U.S. average retail diesel price for the week of 2026-11-02.
  • Down 6.0 cents (0.9%) from last week, a small break for carriers buying fuel at the pump.
  • Up 9.3 cents over four weeks, so surcharges and quotes still need to reflect the higher trend.

Source: EIA: Weekly U.S. diesel price, week of 2026-11-02

CDL Crackdown Spans 15 States

  • 230-plus undocumented immigrants were arrested in a three-day federal and state operation.
  • Over 40 of those arrested held CDLs, signaling tighter scrutiny of driver credentials.
  • Fleets should expect closer checks on driver licensing paperwork during roadside stops.

Source: FreightWaves: 230-plus immigrants arrested in crackdown targeting unauthorized CDL holders

Truckload Margins Still Underwater

  • 109.1% truckload operating ratio at Pamt Corp. in Q3, meaning it spent more than it earned.
  • Driver recruiting costs and a diesel spike drove the loss, pressures small carriers also face.
  • Retailers including Amazon reported paying more for freight in Q3, a sign of firming shipping costs.

Source: FreightWaves: Pamt Corp. reports Q3 net loss, books 109.1% truckload OR · FreightWaves: Amazon, retailers paid more for freight in 3Q

Peak Season and Demand Signals

  • 4% rise in holiday parcel demand is forecast, the strongest peak since 2022.
  • Manufacturing expanded for a 10th straight month, though the Iran war and trade fights weigh on sentiment.
  • Shippers are moving more inventory through inland markets, which could shift where freight originates.

Source: FreightWaves: Parcel shipping demand to increase 4% during holidays, ShipMatrix says · Supply Chain Dive: Manufacturing expands for 10th straight month, but Iran war weighs heavily · Supply Chain Dive: Is warehouse demand shifting towards inland markets?

Equipment and Network Shifts

  • EPA '27 NOx final rule is still pending, and Rush signaled it may not arrive soon.
  • Norfolk Southern and Schneider are expanding East Coast intermodal, adding rail competition on some lanes.
  • FedEx Network 2.0 moves into Los Angeles and other markets after the holidays.

Source: FreightWaves: Waiting on EPA ‘27 rule, Rush says it might not be soon · Supply Chain Dive: Norfolk Southern, Schneider National team to scale East Coast intermodal reach · Supply Chain Dive: FedEx Network 2.0 headed to Los Angeles, other markets post-holidays
