Thursday, November 12, 2026 · NorthStar Freight Brief

Diesel drops 16.5 cents as Trump threatens to double Mexico tariffs

Pump prices ease but stay above $6, a new tariff threat hits cross-border freight, and small-fleet capacity keeps shrinking.

The U.S. average for diesel fell 16.5 cents to $6.127 a gallon, but trade policy just got shakier for cross-border lanes.

Diesel eases but stays above $6

  • $6.127 per gallon: the national diesel average for the week of 2026-11-09, still a heavy weight on cost per mile.
  • Down 16.5 cents (2.6%) week over week, a modest break for carriers running tight margins.
  • Down 5.2 cents over four weeks, so relief is building slowly, not arriving all at once.
  • $6.23/gal at truck stops on Nov. 9, up 65% YoY even as Brent fell 21%, so refining margins are keeping pump prices high.

Source: EIA: Weekly U.S. diesel price, week of 2026-11-09 · FreightWaves: Diesel 101: Why Truck Stop Diesel Is Still Above $6 a Gallon as Crude Falls

Tariff fight heats up again

  • Doubled Mexico tariffs are on the table unless the border migrant increase stops, a direct risk to cross-border freight volumes.
  • Democrats now control both chambers of Congress, adding uncertainty to the trade outlook and shipper planning.
  • Three bills in Congress would sharply limit presidential tariff power, and experts see possible bipartisan support.
  • Manufacturing advocates argue against rolling back trade enforcement, so expect a drawn-out policy fight.

Source: FreightWaves: Trump threatens to double Mexico tariffs over alleged migrant influx · FreightWaves: US midterm shift adds trade uncertainty as trans-Pacific ocean freight rates stay elevated · Supply Chain Dive: Bipartisan push possible to curb Trump tariffs · Supply Chain Dive: Manufacturing advocates make case for tariffs following midterm results

Small-fleet tier keeps shrinking

  • About 362,000 tractors now run with one-to-six-truck carriers, down from a 2023 peak near 398,000.
  • Not all of that is failures; many trucks are moving elsewhere rather than leaving the industry.
  • 15 bankruptcy filings and more than 1,000 job cuts in recent weeks show freight distress is ongoing.

Source: FreightWaves: Why Owner-Operators Are Leaving The Small-Fleet Tier, And Where Their Trucks Go Next · FreightWaves: Freight distress persists as logistics layoffs top 1,000, bankruptcies mount

Ocean imports cool from peak

  • Transpacific ocean rates eased slightly as U.S. port demand slows, though they remain elevated year over year.
  • Imports are coming off a near-record peak, which could thin out port drayage and import-driven loads.
  • Air cargo shows few signs of a late-year volume surge, hinting at a muted holiday freight finish.

Source: Supply Chain Dive: Transpacific ocean rates ease slightly as peak winds down · FreightWaves: US midterm shift adds trade uncertainty as trans-Pacific ocean freight rates stay elevated · Supply Chain Dive: Air cargo’s hopes for end-of-year volume surge dwindle

Rail gains ground on intermodal

  • 6.6% year-over-year growth in U.S. rail traffic, led by intermodal, means more competition for long-haul truckload freight.
  • BLET ended its Wheeling & Lake Erie strike after winning protections for union conductors' work.
  • Genesee & Wyoming plans to split into three rail holding companies and sell dozens of short lines.

Source: FreightWaves: Intermodal leads U.S. rail traffic up 6.6% from a year ago · FreightWaves: BLET ends Wheeling & Lake Erie strike · FreightWaves: Genesee & Wyoming prepares to split into three rail holding companies
