Diesel climbs 9.7 cents to $6.276; fuel tops ATRI's list of trucking concerns

Diesel rebounds after a four-week slide, safety blitzes write hundreds of violations, and a $47M verdict lands in New Mexico.

The national diesel average rose 9.7 cents to $6.276 per gallon, snapping a stretch that still leaves prices down 25.3 cents over four weeks.

Diesel bounces back up

  • $6.276 per gallon is the new U.S. average retail diesel price for the week of 2026-10-19.
  • Up 9.7 cents (1.6%) from last week, so check fuel surcharges on loads booked at older rates.
  • Still down 25.3 cents over four weeks, but the reversal is a reminder to budget for swings.

Source: EIA: Weekly U.S. diesel price, week of 2026-10-19

Fuel is trucking's top worry

  • Fuel prices ranked No. 1 in ATRI's annual top-issues survey after missing the top 10 the prior two years.
  • Drivers ranked fuel first and carriers ranked it second, showing cost pressure is felt across the industry.

Source: FreightWaves: Pump price is trucking’s #1 concern: ATRI survey

Enforcement and fraud crackdowns

  • Hundreds of violations and out-of-service orders came from recent CDL blitzes from California to North Dakota.
  • A Massachusetts man faces up to 15 years over a $3,000 Bluetooth CDL exam cheating scheme.
  • $30.4M in suspected meth was found in a commercial salt load at Laredo, keeping border inspections tight.

Source: FreightWaves: Truck safety blitzes uncover hundreds of violations across US · FreightWaves: Failed CDL exams, $3,000 payments and Bluetooth cheating scheme lead to federal charges · FreightWaves: CBP seizes $30.4M in meth hidden in commercial salt load at Laredo World Trade Bridge

Nuclear verdict in New Mexico

  • $47 million was awarded by a jury to a pickup driver and his mother after a 2022 tanker crash near Carlsbad.
  • Verdicts this size keep pressure on insurance premiums for small fleets.

Source: FreightWaves: New Mexico jury awards $47M in tanker truck crash lawsuit

Labor policy and equipment shifts

  • The 2026 elections will reshape trucking labor policy regardless of which party wins, experts told an ATA committee.
  • Independent contractor rules may no longer split neatly along party lines, a key issue for leased owner-operators.
  • Daimler is building autonomy hardware into Freightliner trucks at the factory while leaving the software choice open.

Source: FreightWaves: The 2026 Elections Will Reshape Trucking Labor Policy No Matter Which Party Wins · FreightWaves: Daimler Is Building Autonomy Into The Truck At The Factory, And Leaving The Software Choice Open
