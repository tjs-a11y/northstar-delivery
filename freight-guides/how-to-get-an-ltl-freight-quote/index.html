How to Get an Accurate LTL Freight Quote

A practical checklist for preparing an LTL freight quote, comparing carrier options and avoiding preventable re-quotes.

Less-than-truckload shipping is useful when your freight does not need an entire trailer. The quote is only as accurate as the shipment details you provide. Before requesting rates, assemble the measurements, handling details and delivery requirements that carriers need to price the move.

Start with the shipment facts

  • Count every pallet or handling unit. Include loose pieces that will travel with the shipment.
  • Measure the loaded footprint and height of each pallet, including overhang and packaging.
  • Record the total shipment weight, not just the product weight.
  • Describe the commodity accurately and flag fragile, hazardous, temperature-sensitive or restricted freight before requesting a quote.

Confirm both ends of the move

  • Use complete pickup and delivery addresses, including ZIP codes and business names.
  • Confirm whether each location has a dock, forklift, liftgate need or limited truck access.
  • Check pickup readiness, receiving hours, appointment requirements and holiday closures.
  • Tell the quoting provider if either end is residential, limited-access, inside-delivery or otherwise nonstandard.

Compare the quote, not just the headline price

  • Check the service level and estimated transit information shown with the rate.
  • Review accessorial charges that may apply to your locations or handling needs.
  • Confirm whether the quote is based on the dimensions, weight and freight description you supplied.
  • Ask what could cause a reclassification, reweigh or post-pickup adjustment.

Verify the details that can change a quote

  • Use the full loaded dimensions of each pallet, including packaging and any overhang.
  • Provide reliable gross weight and an accurate commodity description.
  • Share freight class or NMFC details only when verified; do not guess based on density alone.
  • Disclose stackability, fragile or no-stack restrictions and any unusual shape.
  • Confirm whether accessorials, reweighs or reclassification can change the final charge.

A practical quote checklist

  • Pallet count and dimensions
  • Total weight and commodity description
  • Pickup and delivery ZIP codes and site types
  • Dock, liftgate, appointment and access requirements
  • Ready date and required delivery window

Frequently asked questions

What details matter most for an LTL quote?

Pallet count, dimensions, total weight, commodity, origin, destination and accessorial requirements are essential.

Can an LTL quote change after pickup?

It can if the actual shipment differs from the details used to price it or if additional services are required. Verify measurements and site details before booking.

Does the lowest displayed rate always mean the best choice?

Not necessarily. Compare service details, transit information, accessorial exposure and the carrier option—not only the first price.
