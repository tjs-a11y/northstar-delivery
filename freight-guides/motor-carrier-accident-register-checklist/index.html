Motor Carrier Accident Register Checklist

Federal accident-register duties apply to accidents that meet the regulatory definition. A carrier should record the required facts consistently and keep supporting reports available for the required period.

Important scope note.

Not every incident or vehicle contact automatically meets the federal accident definition. Check 49 CFR 390.5 before deciding whether an event belongs in the register.

Determine whether the event meets the definition

Start with the definition of an accident in 49 CFR 390.5 and the facts of the event. Keep an internal incident record when useful, but do not label every incident a federally reportable accident without checking the applicable definition and exceptions.

Capture the required register fields

Section 390.15 requires the register to identify the accident date, city or nearest location and state, driver name, number of injuries, number of fatalities and whether qualifying hazardous materials were released. Use a consistent form so required details are not lost between dispatch, safety and insurance records.

Keep required supporting reports

Retain copies of accident reports required by state or other governmental entities or insurers. Link each report to the register entry using the event date, driver, vehicle and internal incident reference.

Apply the three-year retention period

Motor carriers must maintain the accident register for three years after the date of each accident. Use the accident date to calculate the retention period and preserve the supporting reports with the related entry.

Make the record available when requested

The regulation also requires carriers to make accident-related records and information available to authorized representatives in an investigation. Keep the file organized, access-controlled and retrievable without changing or recreating the original documents.
