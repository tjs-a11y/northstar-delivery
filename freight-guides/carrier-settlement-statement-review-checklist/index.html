Carrier Settlement Statement Review Checklist

A settlement statement is the final accounting for a load or pay period. Review it against the paperwork you submitted and the terms you accepted—not just the net deposit.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Start with the load identity

Match the broker or carrier load number, pickup and delivery dates, customer or broker name, and equipment details to your rate confirmation. If a statement combines multiple loads, split it into a per-load worksheet before checking totals. A correct total can still hide one underpaid load and one overpaid load.

Reconcile additions before deductions

Check linehaul, fuel surcharge, detention, layover, truck ordered not used (TONU), stop-off, tarp, lumper and other accessorial amounts that were agreed in writing. For each addition, keep the rate confirmation or later written approval beside the supporting receipt, timestamp or facility record.

Read every deduction line

Identify advances, insurance or administrative charges, dispatch fees, factoring fees, escrow or reserve movements, chargebacks and other deductions. Compare each item with the governing broker agreement, lease or other signed terms. Do not assume a deduction is valid—or invalid—just because its label is unfamiliar.

Check the net amount and payment route

Recalculate gross additions less itemized deductions, then compare the expected net to the payment advice and bank or factoring deposit. Confirm whether payment was sent to the carrier or to a factoring company under a notice of assignment. Record the date and amount actually received.

Keep an exception log

For each difference, record the load number, amount, line item, evidence, date raised, contact channel and response. A repeatable log helps identify recurring documentation gaps and makes a later reconciliation less dependent on memory.

Related: source records behind a settlement

For a brokered load, see how to request the broker transaction record under 49 CFR 371.3. For an owner-operator leased to an authorized carrier, review the rated freight bill provisions under 49 CFR 376.12(g).

Keep the next step documented

Save the source documents, the date you submitted them and any response. If the issue involves a contract dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional for advice about the specific facts.
