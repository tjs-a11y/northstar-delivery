LTL vs. Partial Truckload: How to Choose

Understand the practical differences between LTL and partial truckload before choosing a freight option for a multi-pallet shipment.

LTL and partial truckload can both move freight that does not fill a dedicated trailer, but they are not interchangeable. Shipment size, handling sensitivity, route, delivery timing and the way freight is handled all affect which option makes sense.

When LTL may fit

  • Your shipment is relatively small compared with a full trailer.
  • You can provide accurate pallet dimensions, weight and freight description.
  • The freight can move within the carrier's service network and handling process.
  • You are comfortable comparing the quoted service level and any terminal-related handling.

When partial truckload may be worth comparing

  • Your shipment occupies a meaningful amount of trailer space but does not require a full truck.
  • You want to evaluate alternatives to standard LTL handling for a larger or more handling-sensitive load.
  • The lane and pickup/delivery requirements can be matched to available capacity.
  • The quote clearly explains how the shipment will be handled and what services are included.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • How many pallets, linear feet and total pounds are involved?
  • Can the freight be stacked, and are there no-stack or fragile restrictions?
  • Are there strict pickup or delivery appointments?
  • Does the price include the accessorial services your facilities require?
  • What transit estimate and service commitments are actually confirmed?

Make the comparison shipment-specific

  • Request comparable quotes using the same lane and shipment measurements.
  • Check the total price after known accessorials, not only the base rate.
  • Confirm equipment fit, handling expectations and delivery requirements before booking.

Frequently asked questions

Is partial truckload always cheaper than LTL?

No. Pricing depends on lane, capacity, shipment size, timing and service requirements.

Is partial truckload the same as a full truckload?

No. Partial truckload generally uses part of available trailer capacity; a full truckload is a dedicated trailer move.

What information should I prepare?

Pallet count, dimensions, total weight, commodity, origin, destination, loading access and required delivery window.
