How to Reduce Avoidable LTL Freight Reclassification Surprises

The strongest protection against avoidable reclassification is an accurate shipment description supported by measurements and records.

A shipment may be reclassified or re-rated when the information used to quote it does not match the freight tendered or the classification rules applied. The exact process depends on the carrier, tariff and applicable classification. This guide focuses on the shipper's controllable inputs.

Common information gaps

  • Dimensions recorded for the product rather than the full palletized handling unit.
  • Weight that excludes packaging or other components included in the shipment.
  • Generic or inaccurate commodity descriptions.
  • Incorrect pallet count, stackability or handling restrictions.
  • Freight class or NMFC details selected without checking the applicable rule.

Create a pre-pickup verification routine

  1. Measure every handling unit and record the units used.
  2. Weigh the shipment using a reliable method and retain the record.
  3. Describe the commodity specifically and consistently.
  4. Confirm class or NMFC information with a qualified source or the provider.
  5. Match the quote, BOL and packing list to the freight that will actually be tendered.
  6. Photograph the loaded shipment and keep dated records.

If the invoice changes after inspection

Request the inspection or reweigh documentation, the measurement or classification basis, the tariff or rule relied upon and the carrier's dispute process. Compare those records with the shipment data and signed documents. Keep the discussion factual and within the deadlines stated in the governing terms.

Frequently asked questions

Can correct dimensions prevent every reclassification?

No. Accurate dimensions reduce avoidable discrepancies, but classification can depend on other commodity and rule-specific factors.

What records should I keep?

Retain shipment measurements, weight records, commodity descriptions, photos, quote, BOL, packing list and any inspection or reweigh documents.

Should I dispute every re-rating?

Review the supporting records and governing terms first. Dispute only where you have a specific factual or contractual basis.
