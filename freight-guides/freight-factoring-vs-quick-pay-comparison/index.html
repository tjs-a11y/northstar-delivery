Freight Factoring vs. Broker Quick Pay: What Carriers Should Compare

Quick pay and factoring both change when cash reaches a carrier, but they are different arrangements. Compare the complete cost and obligations rather than the advertised speed alone.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Compare a fee with a payment shortfall →

Understand who provides the service

Broker quick pay is an accelerated payment option offered by a broker on that broker's loads. Factoring is a separate financing arrangement in which a factor purchases or advances against eligible receivables under its contract. The details depend on the specific provider and agreement.

Compare the total fee, not just the headline percentage

Check whether the fee applies to linehaul or the full invoice, whether there are minimums, reserves, additional charges, recourse provisions or volume requirements, and when the fee is deducted. A lower headline rate may not mean a lower total cost.

Compare coverage and administration

A broker's quick-pay option may apply only to that broker and may require a separate request for each load. A factor may cover approved customers across multiple brokers, but can impose credit approvals, notice-of-assignment procedures and documentation requirements.

Model the cash-flow trade-off

Use your own invoice amount, fee, expected standard payment date and actual accelerated payment date. Compare the dollars paid for earlier access to cash with the cost of waiting. Avoid assuming that one option is always cheaper or faster.

Read the agreement before choosing

Review termination, recourse, reserve release, customer exclusions and notice requirements. This is general business education, not financial or legal advice; consult the provider and a qualified adviser about your circumstances.

Keep the next step documented

Save the source documents, the date you submitted them and any response. If the issue involves a contract dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional for advice about the specific facts.
