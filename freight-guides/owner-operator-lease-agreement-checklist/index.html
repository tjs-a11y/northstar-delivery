Owner-Operator Lease Agreement Checklist

Before an owner-operator moves equipment under another carrier's authority, the written lease should make the parties' responsibilities and payment terms understandable. Read the actual agreement and any attached addenda before the first trip.

Important scope note.

A lease checklist is a review aid, not a contract template. Part 376 has specific scope and exemptions; the parties and facts matter.

Identify the parties and equipment

Confirm the authorized carrier, equipment owner, equipment identifiers and the person authorized to sign for each party. Make sure the lease describes the equipment and the relationship being documented rather than relying on a dispatch text or verbal promise.

Check the start, end and receipt terms

Review when the lease begins and ends, how possession is transferred and what receipts or interchange records are required. Keep copies of the signed lease, addenda, equipment receipts and any later written changes.

Make the compensation method specific

Check whether compensation is a percentage of gross revenue, a mileage rate, a flat amount or another agreed method. Confirm what revenue or mileage is used, when settlement occurs, how accessorials are treated and whether the calculation is attached to the lease before the trip begins.

Read every deduction and charge-back

List each item that may be deducted from compensation. For each one, look for the calculation method and the documents the carrier must provide so the owner can evaluate whether the charge is valid. Ask for unclear or open-ended deductions to be explained in writing before signing.

Review insurance and other responsibilities

Check which party is responsible for public-liability coverage, physical damage, cargo coverage, occupational accident coverage and other policies that may be relevant. The agreement should identify obligations clearly; the right coverage depends on the operation and applicable rules.

Keep the lease connected to settlements

Save rate confirmations, load numbers, receipts, dispatch approvals and settlement statements together. If a deduction or payment variance appears, compare the written lease and load-specific agreement with the settlement rather than assuming the net deposit explains the difference.

Keep the next step documented

Save the agreement, source records, filing confirmation or written instructions that apply to the specific load or operation. If the question involves a legal dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional about the facts.
