Layover Pay Documentation Checklist for Truck Drivers

A layover usually concerns a delay that extends beyond the same-day loading or unloading window, but definitions and payment conditions vary. Use the written load terms as the starting point.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Review detention, layover and TONU terms →

Distinguish the delay from detention

Read the rate confirmation or applicable carrier agreement to see how it defines detention, layover, rescheduling and overnight delay. Do not apply a standard rate or universal definition from another broker's paperwork.

Record why the truck remained on site or nearby

Keep the original appointment, dispatch instructions, reschedule notice, facility message and the date and time the delay became an overnight issue. Record whether the truck was held at the facility, released to a safe location or redirected.

Get written instructions and approval

Notify dispatch through the required channel and ask what the carrier should do next. Preserve any revised appointment, authorization, layover amount or instruction to remain available. Avoid relying on an undocumented assumption.

Assemble the payment evidence

Submit the load number, original and revised appointments, arrival and release times, messages, receipts if relevant and the exact contract language used for the request. Keep detention and layover calculations separate.

Reconcile the final settlement

When payment arrives, compare the approved layover line with the settlement statement and deposit. If the amount differs, ask for the calculation and log the response. Lease terms, broker contracts and local rules may differ.

Keep the next step documented

Save the source documents, the date you submitted them and any response. If the issue involves a contract dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional for advice about the specific facts.
