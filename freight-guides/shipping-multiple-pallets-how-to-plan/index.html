Shipping Multiple Pallets: Measurements, Planning and Quotes

A step-by-step guide to planning a multi-pallet freight shipment, from measurements and loading details to quote comparison.

Shipping multiple pallets is a coordination exercise. The pallet count, loaded dimensions, total weight, loading sequence and delivery access all influence equipment fit and pricing. A little preparation can prevent delays and re-quotes.

Build a pallet inventory

  • Number each pallet or handling unit.
  • Record loaded length, width and height for each one.
  • Record gross weight per pallet and total shipment weight.
  • Mark stackable, non-stackable, fragile or orientation-sensitive freight.

Plan the loading pattern

  • Identify whether pallets can be loaded in a single row or need a specific sequence.
  • Note overhang, irregular shapes and space that cannot be used.
  • Tell the provider if certain pallets must be unloaded first.
  • Confirm forklift, dock or liftgate availability at each end.

Coordinate pickup and delivery

  • Confirm freight-ready date and loading hours.
  • Verify receiver hours and appointment rules.
  • Share contact names and phone numbers for both facilities.
  • Allow realistic time for loading, unloading and count verification.

Request comparable options

  • Ask whether LTL, partial or full truckload is appropriate for the measured shipment.
  • Use identical shipment details for each comparison.
  • Review accessorials and estimated transit information.
  • Confirm the selected option and schedule before releasing freight.

Frequently asked questions

Is pallet count enough to price a shipment?

No. The provider also needs dimensions, weight, commodity and handling requirements.

Can pallets be stacked to reduce cost?

Only if the freight and packaging safely permit it and the provider accepts the stackability information.

What if the pallets have different sizes?

List each pallet's dimensions and weight rather than using one average measurement.
