Freight Class, Density and Dimensions: A Shipper's Starting Guide

Dimensions and weight are not paperwork details: they help determine how freight is classified, priced and handled.

For many LTL shipments, freight classification can affect the quote. Classification is not determined by density alone in every case; commodity characteristics and applicable classification rules may also matter. Use the carrier or broker's current classification guidance for the specific product.

Measure the handling unit

  • Measure the pallet or package at its widest, longest and highest points, including protrusions.
  • Record the number of identical and non-identical units separately.
  • Use the total shipping weight, including packaging and pallet where applicable.
  • Note whether the freight can be stacked and whether any no-stack restriction applies.

Understand density as one input

Density is commonly calculated as weight divided by volume. For a rectangular handling unit measured in inches, cubic feet can be estimated as length × width × height ÷ 1,728. Divide the weight in pounds by that cubic-foot figure to estimate pounds per cubic foot.

This calculation is a useful description of the shipment, not a substitute for an applicable freight classification. Do not infer a class from density alone unless the governing classification rule specifically permits it.

Provide the commodity details

Give the provider a clear commodity description and any known NMFC item or freight class information. If you do not know the correct classification, say so and request confirmation rather than selecting a guess. Retain the basis for the classification in your shipment records.

Frequently asked questions

Does higher density always mean a lower freight class?

No universal shortcut applies. Density may be relevant, but commodity and classification rules can affect the correct class.

Should I include pallet dimensions?

Yes. Measure the complete handling unit, not only the product inside the carton.

Can I guess freight class to get a quote?

Avoid guessing. Provide accurate product and shipment details and ask the quoting provider to verify the applicable classification.
