ELD Record Retention Checklist for Motor Carriers

ELD recordkeeping is more than downloading a log when asked. A carrier needs a reliable retention and backup process that protects driver information and makes records retrievable.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Retain records of duty status

FMCSA guidance states that a motor carrier must retain ELD records of duty status and supporting documents for six months. Confirm the rule that applies to the record type and retain the underlying files in a form that can be produced when required.

Maintain a separate backup

FMCSA guidance also describes maintaining a backup copy of ELD records on a device separate from the original storage device for the six-month period. Test that the backup can be retrieved; an unverified copy is not a dependable recovery process.

Protect driver privacy

The FMCSA guidance references requirements to retain ELD records in a manner that protects driver privacy. Limit access, use secure storage, track exports and avoid sending full driver records to people who do not need them.

Make records searchable

Use consistent driver identifiers, dates, vehicle references and file naming. Keep supporting documents connected to the relevant duty day so the record can be understood without reconstructing it from scattered messages.

Review the current rule

The federal HOS and ELD requirements contain details and exceptions that are not covered by a short checklist. Check the current text of the regulation and FMCSA guidance for your operation; this is not legal advice.
