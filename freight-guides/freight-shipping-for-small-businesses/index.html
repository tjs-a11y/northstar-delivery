Freight Shipping for Small Businesses: A Practical Guide

A small-business guide to choosing LTL, partial or truckload shipping and preparing accurate freight quote requests.

Small businesses often ship too much for parcel service but not enough to fill a trailer. The challenge is choosing an appropriate freight mode, giving the provider accurate information and understanding the services included in the quote.

Choose the mode around the shipment

Do not use a fixed box count, pallet count or weight as a universal parcel-to-freight rule. Compare actual package dimensions and weight with the parcel provider's current limits, then price LTL or other freight options using the same shipment details.

  • Compare parcel for small individual packages, LTL for smaller palletized freight, partial for larger shared-trailer loads and truckload for a dedicated trailer.
  • Use dimensions and weight—not just the number of boxes—to describe the shipment.
  • Consider fragility, stackability and handling requirements.
  • Ask the provider which options are actually available on your lane.

Prepare before requesting rates

  • Measure every pallet or handling unit after packaging.
  • Record total weight and commodity description.
  • Confirm pickup readiness and receiving hours.
  • Identify dock, liftgate, appointment or limited-access needs.

First-shipment checklist

  • Confirm the commodity description and any special handling requirements.
  • Measure each fully packaged handling unit and record actual gross weight.
  • Check whether freight can be stacked and how it is secured.
  • Confirm dock, forklift, liftgate, appointment and receiving-hour requirements.
  • Keep the quote, BOL, packing list and delivery receipt together.

Build repeatable shipping habits

  • Keep standard pallet dimensions and weights on file where accurate.
  • Photograph freight condition and packaging before handoff when appropriate.
  • Retain quotes, bills of lading, delivery receipts and shipment correspondence.
  • Review accessorials and invoice adjustments to improve future quote accuracy.

Use quotes as a comparison tool

  • Compare multiple service options where available.
  • Check the quoted service, transit estimate and conditions.
  • Ask questions about unclear charges before booking.
  • Keep a record of the final confirmed booking details.

Frequently asked questions

Can a small business use LTL shipping?

Yes. LTL is commonly used when a shipment does not need a full trailer, subject to the freight and lane.

Do I need a freight broker account to get a quote?

Requirements vary by provider. Freight Sidekick currently advertises quote access without needing an account to hear a price.

What is the most common avoidable quoting mistake?

Providing incomplete or inaccurate pallet dimensions, weight or accessorial requirements.
