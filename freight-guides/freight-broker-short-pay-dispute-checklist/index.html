Freight Broker Short-Pay Dispute Checklist

When a freight payment is lower than expected, build the question around one load and one documented difference. A clear evidence packet is more useful than a general complaint.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Confirm the amount that was agreed

Locate the signed rate confirmation and any later written amendments. Verify the load number, linehaul, fuel surcharge, stop count, special handling and accessorial terms. Keep a record of who approved any change and when it was made.

Verify the delivery paperwork

Check that the bill of lading and proof of delivery identify the correct shipment and show delivery completion. Preserve exception notes, signed pages, appointment records and any required facility paperwork. If a document is missing or illegible, identify that gap before sending the dispute.

Match each disputed line to evidence

For detention or layover, assemble arrival and departure timestamps and the notice sent to dispatch. For lumper or other reimbursable charges, include the receipt and the required authorization. For a deduction, identify the exact contract clause or written instruction being questioned.

Send a concise, load-specific inquiry

State the expected amount, the amount paid, the difference and the documents attached. Ask which line item or requirement explains the variance and request the calculation used. Keep the tone factual and save the message, attachments and response.

Track the result without assuming an outcome

Log the date submitted, portal or email confirmation, promised follow-up date and any corrected remittance. Payment rights, dispute procedures and deadlines depend on the contract, lease and applicable law; seek qualified advice when a material dispute remains unresolved.

Keep the next step documented

Save the source documents, the date you submitted them and any response. If the issue involves a contract dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional for advice about the specific facts.
