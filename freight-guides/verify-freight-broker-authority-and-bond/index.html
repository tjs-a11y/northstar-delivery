How to Verify a Freight Broker's Authority and Financial Responsibility

Before accepting a load from an unfamiliar broker, verify the legal entity and authority using the federal source—not just a logo, load-board profile or email signature.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Open the broker authority and bond checker →

Start with the legal business identity

Compare the broker's legal name, address, MC number and contact information across the rate confirmation, carrier packet and public records. Watch for look-alike names, changed payment instructions or email domains that do not match the business.

Use FMCSA Licensing and Insurance

FMCSA directs users to its Licensing and Insurance system to look up interstate operating authority, insurance and process-agent information. Search the exact broker identity and confirm that the record matches the entity offering the load.

Check the authority type and current status

Make sure the record corresponds to broker authority for the service being offered, and review the displayed status and relevant financial-responsibility filing. A carrier's own authority, a broker's authority and a freight forwarder's authority are not interchangeable.

Re-check before relying on old paperwork

Authority and filings can change. Revisit the official record when onboarding a new customer or when a payment, identity or authority detail looks unusual. Save the date and the exact entity searched.

Understand the limit of a lookup

A public record check is one risk-control step, not a guarantee that a load, payment or counterparty is safe. Confirm contractual terms, payment instructions and current contact details independently.

Keep the next step documented

Save the source documents, the date you submitted them and any response. If the issue involves a contract dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional for advice about the specific facts.
