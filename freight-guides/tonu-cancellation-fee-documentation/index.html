TONU Documentation Checklist When a Freight Load Is Canceled

Truck ordered not used (TONU) is a contractual accessorial, not an automatic payment in every cancellation. Preserve the sequence of events and check the actual rate confirmation.

Start with the paperwork.

Keep the load number, signed rate confirmation, dispatch messages, delivery records and settlement together. The written agreement and applicable rules control the result.

Track TONU and detention losses →

Confirm the load was accepted and dispatched

Keep the signed rate confirmation, dispatch acceptance, truck assignment, pickup details and any instructions to proceed. Record whether the driver was already moving, at the shipper or otherwise committed when the cancellation arrived.

Preserve the cancellation timeline

Save the cancellation message, sender, timestamp, stated reason, pickup appointment and the time the driver received the instruction. Note any miles or time already incurred, but do not treat those costs as proof that a TONU amount is owed.

Read the exact TONU clause

Check whether the agreement defines eligibility, exclusions, notice requirements, amount, cap or required approval. Some terms distinguish a canceled load before dispatch from a truck that has already been dispatched or arrived.

Submit the supporting packet

Include the load number, accepted confirmation, dispatch record, cancellation notice, timeline and the clause relied on. Ask for a written explanation if the request is denied or the settlement shows a different amount.

Reconcile the outcome

If a TONU amount is approved, check that it appears on the remittance and is not offset by an unexplained deduction. Contract wording and applicable law control; this checklist is not legal advice.

Keep the next step documented

Save the source documents, the date you submitted them and any response. If the issue involves a contract dispute, regulatory interpretation or a material payment claim, consult a qualified professional for advice about the specific facts.
