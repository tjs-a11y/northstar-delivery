ELD Malfunction Procedure for Truck Drivers and Carriers

An ELD malfunction can affect a driver's ability to display accurate hours-of-service records. The driver and carrier have separate responsibilities, and the response should be documented from the first notice through repair.

Important scope note.

Distinguish an actual malfunction from a data diagnostic event. Follow the current regulation and FMCSA guidance for the specific code and facts.

Driver: document and report the malfunction

FMCSA guidance says the driver must note the malfunction and provide written notice to the motor carrier within 24 hours. Record when the issue was discovered, the device or vehicle involved, the displayed code and when the carrier was notified.

Reconstruct duty-status records when required

If the malfunction prevents accurate recording of hours-of-service data, the driver must reconstruct the current 24-hour period and the previous seven consecutive days unless those records are already available or can be retrieved from the ELD. Use compliant paper records or another permitted recording method until the ELD is back in service.

Carrier: repair or replace within the required window

FMCSA guidance states the carrier must correct, repair, replace or service the malfunctioning ELD within eight days of discovering the condition or receiving the driver's notice, whichever occurs first. The carrier should document the repair request, vendor response, replacement device and return-to-service date.

If an extension may be needed

The regulation provides a process for requesting additional time. FMCSA says the carrier must submit the extension request within five days after the driver notifies the carrier; the request must be signed and sent to the appropriate FMCSA Division Administrator. Check the current requirements before relying on an extension.

Close the event and preserve the records

Keep the written notice, reconstructed logs, carrier response, repair records and any extension correspondence together. Confirm the ELD is operating correctly, review the affected records and follow the carrier's process for certifying and retaining records.
