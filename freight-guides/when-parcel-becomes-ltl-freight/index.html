When Should a Shipment Move From Parcel to LTL Freight?

There is no single pallet count or weight that automatically makes LTL the right choice.

A shipment may be a candidate for LTL when its size, weight, packaging or handling requirements no longer fit the parcel service being considered. Compare the actual shipment against the parcel carrier's limits and an LTL quote rather than relying on a universal threshold.

Signals that a freight quote may be worth comparing

  • Multiple large cartons or palletized units are moving together.
  • Individual packages approach or exceed the parcel provider's size or weight limits.
  • The shipment requires pallet handling, dock coordination or scheduled receiving.
  • The order is replenishment or wholesale freight with a known origin and destination.
  • Parcel surcharges or handling constraints materially change the total cost.

Compare on the same shipment facts

Use the same origin, destination, ready date, package count, dimensions and weight when comparing options. Include residential, liftgate, appointment and other relevant services. Confirm the parcel provider's current limits and the LTL provider's service conditions.

Consider the operational trade-offs

Parcel and LTL services may differ in tracking, terminal handling, pickup arrangements, delivery windows, claims procedures and how freight must be packaged. Choose based on the full service requirement, not only a rough rate comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a universal parcel-to-LTL weight threshold?

No. Limits and pricing vary by carrier, package dimensions, lane and service. Check current provider rules and compare the actual shipment.

Is LTL always cheaper for multiple boxes?

No. The total cost depends on dimensions, weight, class, accessorials and service. Request comparable quotes.

Do I need to palletize freight for LTL?

Many LTL shipments are tendered on pallets, but packaging and handling requirements vary. Confirm the provider's instructions before pickup.
