Owner-Operator Rated Freight Bill Rights Before Settlement

For an owner-operator leased to an authorized motor carrier, the written lease can create specific rights to inspect rate and charge information. The details depend on the lease and the compensation method; this is not a universal rule for every independent carrier or brokered load.

Read the lease rule itself.

49 CFR § 376.12 sets requirements for written leases between authorized carriers and owner-operators. Paragraphs (g) and (h) address rated freight bills, access to rate information and charge-back documentation.

Read 49 CFR § 376.12

When does the rated freight bill provision apply?

Section 376.12(g) addresses a lessor whose compensation is based on a percentage of the gross revenue for a shipment. In that situation, the lease must specify that the authorized carrier will provide the lessor with a copy of the rated freight bill before or at the time of settlement. For a contract carrier, the rule refers to other documents containing the same information.

The same paragraph also requires the lease to permit the lessor to examine the carrier's tariff or other documents used to compute the rate and charges for the shipment. The rule contains a limited allowance for the carrier to delete the shipper's and consignee's names from the copy provided to the lessor.

This provision is about a lessor operating under a lease with an authorized carrier. It should not be presented as an automatic right for every carrier hauling directly for a broker, or as a substitute for the broker transaction-record rule in 49 CFR § 371.3.

What to compare at settlement

  • Load identity: match the rated freight bill to the load number, bill of lading, pickup and delivery dates.
  • Gross revenue basis: check the amount used to calculate the percentage stated in the lease.
  • Rate and accessorials: compare linehaul, fuel surcharge and approved detention, layover, TONU, lumper or other accessorial items with the rate confirmation and supporting documents.
  • Charge-backs: compare every deduction with the categories and terms specified in the lease and the documents supporting the charge.
  • Net settlement: recalculate the percentage, listed deductions and final amount, keeping the source documents with the settlement statement.

Charge-back records matter too

Section 376.12(h) requires the lease to specify items that may be initially paid by the authorized carrier and then charged back to the lessor. The regulation also addresses copies of documents needed to determine the validity of those charge-back items. Read the actual lease language and the current regulation rather than assuming every deduction is allowed or prohibited.

If the settlement includes a charge you do not recognize, identify the line item, amount, date, load and supporting document. Ask for the document that explains the charge and compare it with the lease. Keep the request and response in writing.

A practical document request

Use a written request that identifies the load and settlement period. Ask for the rated freight bill or equivalent rate document required by the lease, the tariff or other rate-computation material available under § 376.12(g), and the supporting documents for any charge-back under § 376.12(h). Keep a copy of the lease, rate confirmation, bill of lading, proof of delivery, receipts, settlement statement and correspondence.

Do not assume the carrier must disclose every commercial term or an unredacted shipper identity. The rule expressly allows certain names to be deleted from the copy. If a material dispute remains, get advice from a transportation attorney familiar with the lease and the specific facts.
