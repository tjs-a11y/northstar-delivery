Shipper guide

Dry van freight: the workhorse of American shipping.

A dry van is an enclosed, non-refrigerated trailer, most often 53 feet long. It carries palletized and boxed freight that needs protection from weather but not temperature control: retail goods, packaged food, paper, building supplies and more.

Make your quote accurate

  • Pallet count and size: standard or oversized pallets, and whether they can be stacked.
  • Weight: total load weight. Heavy loads may limit how much can be carried.
  • Live or drop: a live load means the truck waits while you load or unload. A drop means the trailer is left for you to load on your own time. Drop service depends on equipment availability.
  • Dock or no dock: without a loading dock you may need a liftgate or a forklift on site.
  • Team service: two drivers for time-critical freight. It costs more, but the truck keeps moving.

Avoid the common extra charges

  • Detention: be ready at the appointment time, so the driver isn't held beyond the free time.
  • Truck ordered, not used: if the load cancels, tell the broker as early as possible.
  • Re-delivery: confirm that the receiver's hours and appointment requirements are in the order.

Questions

Is a dry van the same as a box truck?

No. A dry van is a semi-trailer pulled by a tractor. Box trucks are smaller straight trucks used mostly for local delivery.

Do I need a full truckload?

Not always. If your freight fills only part of a trailer, ask about partial or LTL options.
