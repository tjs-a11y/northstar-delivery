Flatbed freight: for loads that don't fit through a door.

A flatbed is an open trailer with no sides or roof, loaded by crane or forklift from the side or top. It carries steel, lumber, pipe, building materials, machinery and other freight that is too big, heavy or awkward for a van.

Choose the right open-deck trailer

  • Flatbed: the standard open deck, typically 48 to 53 feet.
  • Flatbed with sides: adds removable side panels for loads that need containment.
  • Step deck: a lower rear deck for taller freight. See heavy haul and oversized freight.
  • Conestoga: a flatbed with a rolling tarp system. It loads like a flatbed but covers like a van.

What to include in your request

  • Exact dimensions (length, width, height) and weight of each piece.
  • Whether the load needs tarps (for weather protection) and what type.
  • How it will be loaded and unloaded: forklift, crane, or loading ramp.
  • Any securement needs: chains, straps, edge protectors, dunnage.

Questions

Does flatbed freight need tarps?

It depends on the product. Steel coils, lumber and finished goods often need tarps, while pipe or concrete products may not. Say so in your quote request, because tarping is often priced separately.

What if my load is taller or wider than normal?

You may need a step deck, RGN or an oversize permit. See our heavy haul guide.
